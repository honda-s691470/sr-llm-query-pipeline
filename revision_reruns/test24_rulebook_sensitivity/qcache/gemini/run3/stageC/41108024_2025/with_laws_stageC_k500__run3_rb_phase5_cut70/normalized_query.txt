Background: This systematic review and meta-analysis aimed to evaluate the effectiveness of various analgesic therapies for managing rheumatoid arthritis-related pain symptoms. This pseudo abstract is optimized for retrieval of likely in-scope biomedical studies.
Methods: We conducted a structured evidence study aligned to the review question, with eligibility criteria defined as follows: Adult patients (18+) diagnosed with rheumatoid arthritis experiencing disease-related pain; human studies only. Studies had to assess the effectiveness of analgesic treatments, including pharmaceutical therapies (NSAIDs, opioids, acetaminophen/paracetamol, COX-2 inhibitors, biologics, corticosteroids) and non-pharmaceutical therapies (physical therapy, neural training, functional training, occupational therapy, ergotherapy, cognitive behavioral therapy, hypnotherapy, ultrasound therapy, acupuncture, thermotherapy, laser therapy, electrotherapy, balneotherapy). Eligible comparisons included drug versus drug, drug versus non-pharmaceutical intervention, drug versus placebo, non-pharmaceutical versus non-pharmaceutical, and non-pharmaceutical versus placebo. Outcomes had to relate to pain relief, pain management, pain severity scores, reduction of pain intensity, or maintenance of pain symptoms. All study durations and follow-ups were eligible. Eligible designs were randomized controlled trials, cohort studies (prospective and retrospective), and case-controlled studies. No publication-date limits were applied; English-language articles and English abstracts of non-English articles were reviewed. Out-of-scope studies were excluded using these constraints: Patients with non-rheumatoid-arthritis-related pain; mixed patient populations where rheumatoid-arthritis-specific data were not clearly distinguished; studies focused on treatments unrelated to pain management or not using analgesic therapies, including DMARDs that do not primarily target pain; studies without a comparison between treatments or interventions; studies that did not report pain-related outcomes; case series, case reports, editorials, opinion pieces, letters, animal studies, in vitro studies, and abstracts without a published study manuscript. The Methods section also states that studies were excluded if they were non-comparative, involved non-human subjects, or if pain outcomes could not be clearly attributed to active rheumatoid arthritis without other comorbidities affecting pain relief.
Results: Candidate studies meeting these criteria showed consistent directional effects across key outcomes relevant to the review question, and reported findings in a clinically interpretable way without relying on unsupported exact statistics.
Conclusions: The findings support relevance to the review objective and justify prioritization for downstream screening under the predefined inclusion criteria.
