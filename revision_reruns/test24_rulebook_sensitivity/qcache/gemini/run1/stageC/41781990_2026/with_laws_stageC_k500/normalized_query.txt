Background: To systematically evaluate, by meta-analysis, the safety of hyperthermic intraperitoneal chemotherapy (HIPEC) in treating gastrointestinal malignancies, addressing a central clinical concern that often dictates its adoptability independent of efficacy considerations, and to provide evidence-based medical support for its clinical application. This pseudo abstract is optimized for retrieval of likely in-scope biomedical studies.
Methods: We conducted a structured evidence study aligned to the review question, with eligibility criteria defined as follows: English-language randomized controlled trials (RCTs); patients diagnosed with advanced gastric or colorectal cancer with peritoneal metastases; HIPEC as the experimental intervention; a non-HIPEC control group; and studies reporting relevant data on the incidence of adverse reactions or complications. Out-of-scope studies were excluded using these constraints: Excluded were studies in patients without gastric or colorectal cancer; studies not using HIPEC; non-randomized controlled trials, case analyses, review articles, protocols, pilot studies, single-arm clinical trials, and multi-arm trials lacking a control group; and studies with incomplete outcome indicators, unobtainable data, or data that could not be converted.
Results: Candidate studies meeting these criteria showed consistent directional effects across key outcomes relevant to the review question, and reported findings in a clinically interpretable way without relying on unsupported exact statistics.
Conclusions: The findings support relevance to the review objective and justify prioritization for downstream screening under the predefined inclusion criteria.
