Background: To provide a qualitative synthesis of what is known regarding depression symptoms among the South Asian diaspora in English-speaking countries, exploring reported symptoms, comparing them with ICD-11 diagnostic criteria, and considering acculturation and ethnic differences. This pseudo abstract is optimized for retrieval of likely in-scope biomedical studies.
Methods: We conducted a structured evidence study aligned to the review question, with eligibility criteria defined as follows: Original qualitative, quantitative and/or mixed-method primary research, plus eligible grey literature (local NHS trust reports and guides, government reports and briefings, and educational theses), written in English, involving adults (18+) from the South Asian diaspora (Afghan, Bangladeshi, Bhutanese, Indian, Maldivian, Nepalese, Pakistani or Sri Lankan heritage) living in the UK, USA, Canada, Australia and/or New Zealand, where reported findings included details of symptoms among participants experiencing depression (diagnosed or self-identified), or mental distress where authors identified symptoms as potential indicators of depression. Out-of-scope studies were excluded using these constraints: Exclude studies in which South Asian-specific findings could not be extracted from wider demographic categories or samples including non-South-Asian participants whose results could not be isolated; non-English sources; comorbidity studies in which depression symptoms were considered alongside another condition (for example diabetes or cancer); studies focused solely on postpartum depression; and quantitative studies that did not report symptoms item by item.
Results: Candidate studies meeting these criteria showed consistent directional effects across key outcomes relevant to the review question, and reported findings in a clinically interpretable way without relying on unsupported exact statistics.
Conclusions: The findings support relevance to the review objective and justify prioritization for downstream screening under the predefined inclusion criteria.
