Background: This systematic review examines the effects of yerba mate preparations consumed in its various traditional and commercial forms and, secondarily, of its main isolated bioactive constituents on major pathogenic mechanisms of RA, including chronic inflammation, oxidative stress, FLS activation, joint damage, bone remodeling, and pain modulation. This pseudo abstract is optimized for retrieval of likely in-scope biomedical studies.
Methods: We conducted a structured evidence study aligned to the review question, with eligibility criteria defined as follows: We included original studies in humans, human-derived cells, or animal models that evaluated Ilex paraguariensis preparations, such as traditional infusions, aqueous or hydroalcoholic extracts, or commercial formulations, or isolated bioactive constituents recognised as major components of yerba mate (including chlorogenic and caffeoylquinic acids, quercetin, and rutin). Eligible designs comprised randomized or non-randomized clinical trials, observational studies, human-based in vitro investigations, and in vivo animal studies using established models of arthritis, inflammation, or oxidative stress. Studies were required to report at least one outcome relevant to RA, including clinical parameters (e.g., DAS28, CRP, ESR), systemic inflammatory or oxidative biomarkers, or mechanistic endpoints involving RA-related pathways such as NF-kappaB, JAK/STAT, Nrf2 activation, fibroblast-like synoviocyte behaviour, cytokine release, or alterations in the RANKL/OPG axis. To ensure up-to-date evidence, the review applied a 10-year window for yerba mate preparation studies and a 5-year window for isolated bioactive-compound studies. Out-of-scope studies were excluded using these constraints: We excluded animal studies investigating isolated compounds, non-human in vitro models, non-original articles, studies lacking primary data or RA-relevant outcomes, grey literature, articles without full text available despite reasonable retrieval attempts, articles not published in English or Spanish, and any study outside the predefined time windows.
Results: Candidate studies meeting these criteria showed consistent directional effects across key outcomes relevant to the review question, and reported findings in a clinically interpretable way without relying on unsupported exact statistics.
Conclusions: The findings support relevance to the review objective and justify prioritization for downstream screening under the predefined inclusion criteria.
