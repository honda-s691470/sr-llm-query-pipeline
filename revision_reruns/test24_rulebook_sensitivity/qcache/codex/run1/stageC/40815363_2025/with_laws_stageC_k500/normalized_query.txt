Background: To elucidate whether probiotic oral bacteriotherapy attenuates oxidative/nitrosative stress, oxidative biomolecule damage, and inflammation in patients with non-communicable diseases. This pseudo abstract is optimized for retrieval of likely in-scope biomedical studies.
Methods: We conducted a structured evidence study aligned to the review question, with eligibility criteria defined as follows: Randomized controlled trials in patients with non-communicable diseases characterized by inflammatory processes and oxidative stress; clearly defined probiotic intervention (single- or multi-strain) with dose and duration specified; placebo control; viable outcome data for oxidative/nitrosative stress or related inflammatory markers; adequate quantitative data (MD or SMD with SD/SE); clear diagnostic criteria; and at least three studies reporting at least one shared primary oxidative/nitrosative stress outcome. Out-of-scope studies were excluded using these constraints: Studies not relevant to the topic or not reporting the clinical outcomes of interest; missing protocol or full text; non-viable primary/secondary endpoint data; non-RCTs or trials without a control group; animal or in vitro studies; non-NCD populations (including healthy, pregnant, overweight/obese participants); infectious diseases and inflammatory bowel diseases; complex chronic conditions with unclear underlying cause; probiotic interventions combined with another nutraceutical or unclear microbial composition; and active-control/placebo regimens that also contained probiotics, prebiotics, or other agents.
Results: Candidate studies meeting these criteria showed consistent directional effects across key outcomes relevant to the review question, and reported findings in a clinically interpretable way without relying on unsupported exact statistics.
Conclusions: The findings support relevance to the review objective and justify prioritization for downstream screening under the predefined inclusion criteria.
