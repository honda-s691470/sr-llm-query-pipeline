Background: To obtain an updated view of the available prognostic models for heart failure patients; specifically, to review multivariable regression and machine learning models predicting heart failure progression and, when enough studies were available, quantitatively synthesize predictive performance and identify sources of heterogeneity across mortality, hospitalization, and composite outcomes.
Methods: We conducted a structured evidence study aligned to the review question, with eligibility criteria defined as follows: Population: adult human subjects with heart failure, without a defined ethnicity; each primary study's adult-age definition and heart-failure definition were accepted. Index model: multivariable regression models and machine learning techniques for predicting heart failure progression on the basis of demographic, socioeconomic, and clinical prognostic factors, including studies that developed new multivariable prognostic models and studies that performed external validation of existing models. Outcome: predictive performance for overall mortality, HF-related and all-cause hospitalizations, emergency department access, and stroke as a major adverse cardiovascular event within 5 years of inclusion; all performance measures were eligible for the systematic review, but meta-analysis was feasible only for the C-statistic. Timing: 1 to 5 years. Setting: prediction models intended to be used by healthcare professionals at any time during heart failure progression. Eligible designs included cohort and case-control studies and observational studies incorporating cohorts from randomized controlled trials. Non-English articles were eligible, and abstracts/proceedings were evaluated for inclusion.
Results: Candidate studies meeting these criteria showed consistent directional effects across key outcomes, with quantitative estimates and uncertainty reported.
Conclusions: The findings support relevance to the review objective and justify further validation in larger and externally evaluated datasets.
