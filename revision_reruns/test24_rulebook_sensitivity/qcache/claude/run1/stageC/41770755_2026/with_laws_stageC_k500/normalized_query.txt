Background: To systematically synthesise the published evidence on the feasibility, acceptability, and effectiveness of digitally delivered multimodal prehabilitation interventions for cancer patients; specifically, to evaluate feasibility and acceptability, assess effects on patient outcomes such as physical fitness, psychological readiness, nutritional status, and quality of life, and explore patient and provider perspectives on using these interventions in cancer care.
Methods: We conducted a structured evidence study aligned to the review question, with eligibility criteria defined as follows: Primary quantitative, qualitative, or mixed-methods studies of digitally delivered multimodal prehabilitation for cancer patients. Eligible participants were adults aged 18 years or older with a cancer diagnosis and/or healthcare professionals involved in cancer care. Interventions had to focus on the prehabilitation phase before primary treatment (for example surgery, chemotherapy, or radiotherapy), include all three components of multimodal prehabilitation (physical, nutritional, and psychological), and be delivered digitally (for example via wearable fitness trackers, telehealth platforms, or mobile health applications). Outcomes of interest included feasibility, acceptability, physical fitness, psychological readiness, nutritional status, and quality of life. Searches were limited to peer-reviewed English-language studies published from January 2008 onward.
Results: Candidate studies meeting these criteria showed consistent directional effects across key outcomes, with quantitative estimates and uncertainty reported.
Conclusions: The findings support relevance to the review objective and justify further validation in larger and externally evaluated datasets.
